# Module 2: Electronic Components

## Overview

Welcome to Module 2 of the Raspberry Pi Robotics & IoT Crash Course!

In this module, you will explore the electronic components commonly used in robotics and physical computing projects. You will learn what each component does, how it works, how to identify its terminals and how to connect it safely to a Raspberry Pi-based project.

This module builds on the GPIO fundamentals from Module 1 and prepares you for the robotic gripper and two-wheeled robot projects.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will be able to:

- Identify common electronic components used in robotics.
- Understand the basic working principles of servo motors and DC gear motors.
- Explain why a motor driver is required for DC motors.
- Understand how an ultrasonic sensor measures distance.
- Recognise the importance of suitable power supplies and common ground.
- Read basic component specifications and identify electrical limits.
- Select appropriate components for simple robotics projects.

- Batteries and suitable power supplies
- Jumper wires and breadboards

Some components were introduced in Module 1. This module focuses on understanding their purpose and electrical requirements in a larger circuit.

## Hardware Requirements

The exact parts depend on the activities you choose to complete.

- Raspberry Pi 3B+, Raspberry Pi 4 or Raspberry Pi 5
- Breadboard and jumper wires
- Servo motor
- DC gear motors
- Compatible motor driver
- Ultrasonic distance sensor
- Suitable external power supply
- Resistors for voltage-divider or signal-protection circuits, where required

## Safety Guidelines

- Disconnect power before changing circuit connections.
- Never connect a motor directly to a Raspberry Pi GPIO pin.
- Do not power motors or servos from a GPIO pin.
- Check the voltage and current requirements of every component.
- Use a suitable motor driver and power supply.
- Connect the Raspberry Pi ground and external supply ground when the circuit requires a shared reference.
- Raspberry Pi GPIO pins use 3.3V logic and are not 5V tolerant.
- Check the ultrasonic sensor's Echo output voltage before connecting it to a GPIO pin.
